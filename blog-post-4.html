System Audio vs Microphone: Complete Recording Guide

✍️ Waqar Ahmed 📅 September 2026 ⏱️ 5 min read

One of the most common questions I get about screen recording is: "Should I enable system audio or microphone?" The answer isn't always obvious, and getting it wrong means either missing important sounds or ruining your recording with unwanted noise.

In this complete guide, I'll explain the difference between system audio and microphone, when to use each one, and how to combine them for professional-quality recordings.

What is System Audio?

System audio (also called "computer audio," "internal audio," or "desktop audio") is all the sound your computer produces through its speakers or headphones. It includes:

  • Game sound effects and music
  • Video audio from YouTube, Netflix, etc.
  • Music playing from Spotify, iTunes, etc.
  • Notification sounds from apps
  • Voices of other participants in Zoom/Meet calls
  • Sound effects from software and websites
  • System sounds (clicks, alerts, errors)

When you enable system audio in a screen recorder, you capture everything your computer "hears" internally. Nothing from your physical environment is captured — only digital sounds.

What is Microphone Audio?

Microphone audio is sound captured from your physical environment through your device's microphone. It includes:

  • Your voice (commentary, narration)
  • Ambient sounds in your room (fan noise, traffic, keyboard clicks)
  • Other people in the room
  • Acoustic instruments or live sounds
  • Pets, doors, phone notifications from other devices

When you enable microphone in a screen recorder, you capture everything your microphone physically picks up. The quality depends heavily on your microphone hardware.

The Big Difference

Feature System Audio Microphone
Source Digital (from computer) Physical (from room)
Captures Game sounds, videos, other people Your voice, ambient noise
Quality Perfect (digital signal) Depends on mic hardware
Background noise None Possible (fans, traffic, etc.)
Best for Games, videos, meetings Commentary, tutorials, vlogs

When to Use System Audio Only

Enable only system audio (turn off microphone) in these scenarios:

  • Recording YouTube videos: You want the video's audio, not your voice
  • Capturing gameplay highlights: Pure game audio without commentary
  • Recording music videos: Clean audio from the source
  • Archiving meetings silently: When you don't want to add your voice
  • Recording presentations: Only the presenter's voice from the call
  • Saving webinars: Clean audio without your surroundings
💡 Pro Tip: Use headphones when recording system audio only. This prevents the mic (even if disabled) from picking up speaker output if your OS is configured that way.

When to Use Microphone Only

Enable only microphone (turn off system audio) in these scenarios:

  • Recording vlogs: Your voice is the main content
  • Voice-overs for videos: Adding narration to existing footage
  • Podcast recording: Voice-only content
  • Recording your own screen silently: When you don't need system sounds
  • Dictation and notes: Voice memos while screen recording

When to Use BOTH (System Audio + Microphone)

Enable both for the most professional recordings. This is the ideal setup for:

  • Gaming with commentary: Game audio + your reactions = engaging content
  • Tutorial videos: Screen content audio + your explanations
  • Zoom/Meet recordings: Other participants + your voice
  • Software demos: App sounds + your narration
  • Reaction videos: Source audio + your reactions
  • Educational content: Video audio + teacher's voice

Common Audio Problems and Fixes

Problem 1: Echo or Feedback

Symptom: You hear your own voice repeated back to you in the recording.

Cause: Your microphone is picking up the sound coming from your speakers, creating a feedback loop.

Fix: Always use headphones when recording with both audio sources. This isolates your mic from the system audio output.

Problem 2: System Audio Too Loud, Voice Too Quiet

Symptom: Game or video audio drowns out your commentary.

Fix: Lower your system volume (or the app's volume) to about 60-70%, and speak clearly into the microphone. Test the balance with a short recording first.

Problem 3: Background Noise in Microphone

Symptom: Fan noise, traffic, or keyboard clicks in your recording.

Fix: Move to a quieter room, use a directional microphone, or enable echo cancellation and noise suppression in your OS audio settings.

Problem 4: No System Audio in Recording

Symptom: You enabled system audio but the recording has no game/video sound.

Cause: In the browser popup, the "Share audio" checkbox wasn't ticked.

Fix: Restart the recording. In the popup, look for a "Share audio" or "Share tab audio" checkbox at the bottom-left and make sure it's checked before clicking Share.

Problem 5: No Microphone Audio

Symptom: Your voice is missing from the recording.

Fix: Check browser permissions — the first time you use the recorder, your browser may ask for microphone access. Click "Allow." Also verify the correct microphone is selected in your OS audio settings.

Audio Quality Tips

  • Use a dedicated microphone: Even a $20 USB mic beats your laptop's built-in mic
  • Position matters: Keep the mic 6-12 inches from your mouth, slightly off-axis
  • Pop filter: Reduces harsh "P" and "B" sounds
  • Speak at consistent volume: Don't whisper one minute and shout the next
  • Test before recording: 30 seconds of test audio saves hours of frustration
  • Use headphones: Prevents echo and lets you monitor your audio in real time
  • Quiet room: Close windows, turn off fans, silence notifications

Conclusion

Understanding the difference between system audio and microphone is the key to producing professional recordings. Use system audio for game sounds and video content, microphone for your voice, and both for the most engaging content.
