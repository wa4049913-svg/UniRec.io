System Requirements for UniRecorder — Best Settings for Low-End PC (2026 Guide)

By Waqar Ahmed · September 21, 2026 · 8 min read

Not every PC can handle 4K or 2K screen recording. If your computer is low-end — no dedicated GPU, less RAM, older CPU — recording in high resolution will cause lag, crashes, and broken recordings. This guide explains exactly what settings to use, and what to avoid.

⚠️ Important: If your PC is low-end, do not record in 4K or 2K. Your recording will stutter, drop frames, or crash the browser entirely. Stick to 720p at 30 FPS for smooth results.

What Is UniRecorder?

UniRecorder is a free browser-based screen recorder. Because it runs inside Chrome, Edge, or Firefox, its performance depends on your system resources — CPU, RAM, and (optionally) GPU. Unlike desktop apps like OBS, it doesn't install anything, but it also can't use advanced hardware encoding in every case.

That means: the weaker your PC, the more carefully you need to choose your settings.

Why You Should NOT Record in 4K on a Low-End PC

This is the most common mistake users make. Here's why 4K recording fails on weak hardware:

  • RAM overflow: 4K video uses roughly 4x more RAM than 1080p. On a 4 GB PC, the browser tab will crash.
  • CPU bottleneck: Encoding 4K needs serious CPU power. A dual-core CPU will drop frames continuously.
  • No GPU support: Without hardware encoding, everything runs on the CPU — the recording will lag badly.
  • File size explosion: 1 minute of 4K recording can be 300 MB or more. Low-end PCs with slow HDDs will struggle to save it.
  • Browser crashes: Chrome tabs have memory limits. 4K recording hits that limit fast.

⚠️ Warning: If your PC has less than 8 GB RAM or no dedicated GPU, never select 4K or 2K. Your recording will either fail or be unusable.

Signs Your PC Can't Handle 4K Recording

Stop recording immediately if you notice any of these:

  • Preview becomes choppy or freezes
  • Fan spins loudly and PC heats up
  • Mouse cursor lags behind your movement
  • Browser tab shows "Page Unresponsive"
  • Recording timer freezes or jumps
  • The final video has audio desync or missing frames

If any of these happen, lower your settings immediately:

  1. Stop the current recording
  2. Change resolution to 720p
  3. Change FPS to 30
  4. Disable webcam overlay
  5. Close all other browser tabs and apps
  6. Restart the browser

How to Fix Recording Lag on Low-End PC

Follow these steps in order. Most users see instant improvement after step 2 or 3.

1. Close Background Apps

Discord, Steam, Spotify, and even Chrome extensions eat RAM. Before recording, close everything you don't need. Check Task Manager (Ctrl+Shift+Esc on Windows) — RAM usage should be under 50%.

2. Lower Resolution to 720p

720p is 4x lighter than 1080p and 9x lighter than 4K. If your PC is weak, 720p at 30 FPS is the sweet spot.

3. Set FPS to 30 (Not 60)

60 FPS doubles the encoding load. For low-end PCs, 30 FPS is perfectly fine for tutorials, meetings, and most gameplay.

4. Disable Webcam Overlay

The webcam overlay requires merging two video streams in real time. On weak hardware, this doubles CPU usage. Turn it off if your PC struggles.

5. Use Chrome or Edge (Not Firefox)

Chrome and Edge use hardware acceleration better. Firefox may work, but performance is usually lower for screen recording.

6. Record Shorter Clips

Instead of recording a 30-minute session, record 5-minute chunks. This reduces RAM buildup and prevents crashes.

7. Plug In Your Laptop

Battery mode throttles the CPU. Always plug in your laptop before recording — you'll get full CPU speed.

8. Close Other Browsers and Tabs

Keep only one browser tab open: the UniRecorder tab. Everything else should be closed.

Does UniRecorder Work on Old Laptops?

Yes — but with restrictions. On a laptop with 4 GB RAM and integrated graphics, you can record at 720p / 30 FPS smoothly for up to 10 minutes. Anything higher will fail.

This is not a limitation of UniRecorder itself — it's a limitation of the hardware. Every browser-based recorder faces the same issue.

Can I Record 4K if I Have a Dedicated GPU?

Yes, but only if:

  • You have 16 GB RAM or more
  • Your GPU supports hardware video encoding (NVENC, AMD VCE, Intel Quick Sync)
  • Your browser supports hardware acceleration (Chrome and Edge do)
  • You close all other apps

Even then, 4K recording may still lag if your CPU is old. Test with a 1-minute recording first before committing to a long session.

✅ Best practice: Always do a 10-second test recording before starting a long session. If the test is smooth, proceed. If not, lower your settings.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I record in 4K on a low-end PC?

No. If your PC has less than 16 GB RAM, no dedicated GPU, or an old CPU, 4K recording will crash or lag badly. Stick to 720p at 30 FPS.

What are the minimum system requirements for UniRecorder?

4 GB RAM, dual-core CPU, and a modern browser (Chrome, Edge, Firefox). For 1080p recording, 8 GB RAM is recommended.

Why does my recording lag or crash?

Usually because of 4K/2K settings on weak hardware, too many tabs open, low RAM, or high FPS. Lower to 720p and 30 FPS.

Is 1080p better than 4K for low-end PC?

Yes. 1080p at 30 FPS is much lighter. For really weak PCs, use 720p at 30 FPS.

Does recording use a lot of RAM?

Yes. Higher resolution and longer recordings use more RAM. 4K recordings can use several GB and crash low-end PCs.

How do I record my screen without lag on a weak PC?

Close background apps, use 720p at 30 FPS, disable webcam, record shorter clips, and use Chrome or Edge.
